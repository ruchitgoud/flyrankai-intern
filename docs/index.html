Content Refresh Prioritization

Can observable content and search-performance signals help prioritize pages that may be declining, so a content team can decide which pages deserve human review first?

Decision supportClient-grouped validationInterpretable Decision TreePublic-safe analysis

Abstract

This study asks whether observable content and search-performance signals can help prioritize pages that may be declining for human review. The ML evaluation uses an anonymized 30,000-row content dataset and treats trend_direction = down as a proxy label. I compared a transparent Week-4 rule-based baseline with an interpretable Decision Tree using a client-grouped holdout and excluded target-derived fields from the predictive features. On the recorded holdout, the Decision Tree achieved ROC AUC 0.6392 and Average Precision 0.6105, compared with 0.5104 and 0.5207 for the Week-4 baseline. The result suggests useful predictive signal for prioritization, but it does not establish that refreshing content causes traffic recovery, so the output is intended for human-reviewed decision support.

1. Introduction / Problem

Content teams often have more pages to review than they can investigate deeply. The practical problem is therefore not simply identifying whether a page is “good” or “bad,” but deciding which pages deserve attention first.

This project focuses on Lane 2: refresh/content opportunity scoring. The goal is to combine observable signals into a ranked review queue that helps a content team focus limited review effort. The ranking is a prioritization aid, not an automatic content-change system.

The internship warehouse contains daily content-performance data at daily × client × content grain, alongside content and query tables. The ML exercise uses the provided anonymized starter dataset so the evaluation is reproducible without exposing private client data.

The target is a proxy: is_declining_label = 1 when trend_direction == "down". IDs are grouping/join context only. trend_direction, trend_pct, and product/output fields that could encode the outcome are excluded from model features.

The broader FlyRank study uses rolling performance windows and explicitly treats the data as observational; missing tracking and uneven client history are handled as data-quality/context considerations rather than interpreted as zero activity.

3. Methodology

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline is a transparent score combining content staleness with CTR weakness relative to search position.

Model

An interpretable Decision Tree was chosen because it can learn combinations of observable signals while remaining easy to inspect.

Validation

Clients were split into training and test groups so no test client appeared in training. The recorded split contains 26,581 training rows across 25 clients and 3,419 test rows across 7 clients, with zero client overlap.

Leakage controls

The workflow excludes target-derived trend fields, the target itself, identifiers, and product decision outputs such as health/priority/action fields when present. The audit explicitly checks that these fields are not used as predictive features.

The Decision Tree improved ROC AUC by about 0.129 and Average Precision by about 0.090 on the recorded holdout. This is evidence of stronger predictive separation for the proxy label under the evaluated setup.

Honest interpretation: the result does not prove that a content refresh causes recovery, improves traffic, or guarantees a business outcome. It supports using the model as a prioritization signal that still requires human review.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The target is a proxy for observed decline, not a direct future-success outcome.
  • The data is observational; associations do not establish causation.
  • Client-grouped validation improves the evaluation design for cross-client generalization but is not a guarantee for every future client.
  • The starter ML dataset is narrower than the full warehouse.
  • No randomized or causal refresh experiment was performed.
  • A ranking can be wrong when search intent, seasonality, recent changes, business context, or data quality is not represented.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. STALE_AND_LOW_CTR — review pages that are both stale and weak on CTR relative to search-position context.
  2. STALE_CONTENT — review stale pages with enough visibility to justify investigation.
  3. LOW_CTR_FOR_POSITION — investigate pages whose CTR is weak for their ranking context.
  4. GENERAL_REVIEW — use remaining signals as a lower-priority review queue.

Human review is required

Reviewers should check search intent, relevance, recent changes, the likely reason behind the observed signal, action suitability, and how the outcome will be measured.

No automatic publishing, rewriting, deletion, or redirection should be triggered by the score alone.
